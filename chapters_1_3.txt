Chapter ONE
Introduction
1.1 Background to the Study
It is now expected from educated individuals that they not only have theoretical knowledge but also be able to practice it through industry related skills. In order to bridge this gap between education and practice, especially in the case of Nigeria, the Students Industrial Work Experience Scheme (SIWES) 
was
 started. This scheme provides students with industrial experience where they learn workplace behavior and technical knowledge.
Despite the importance of SIWES, many institutions still rely on manual methods in managing and monitoring students participating in the 
program
. Students are usually expected to keep daily records of activities in logbooks, while supervisors visit organizations physically for inspection and assessment. Reports are often submitted manually at the end of the 
program
. This traditional method has several limitations, especially when students are posted to different locations across the country.
The manual system of managing SIWES activities is often slow, stressful, and inefficient. Supervisors may find it difficult to monitor a large number of students in different organizations. Students may forget to update their logbooks regularly or may lose important records. There may also be delays in communication between students, institutions, and supervisors. In some cases, students may not receive timely feedback concerning their progress.
Another challenge of the manual method is poor record management. Physical documents can be misplaced, damaged, or manipulated. Retrieving information from paper files may be difficult and time-consuming. Institutions may also find it hard to generate accurate reports on students’ placements, attendance, activities, and performance. These problems reduce the effectiveness of the SIWES 
program
.
The advancement in information and communication technology has made it possible to solve many administrative and academic challenges through digital systems. Educational institutions now adopt web-based and mobile-based systems to improve efficiency, transparency, and accessibility. Applying technology to SIWES management can significantly improve the process of monitoring and evaluating students during industrial training.
A Student SIWES Activity Tracking System is a computerized platform designed to help students, supervisors, and administrators manage SIWES activities electronically. The system can allow students to register, update their place of attachment, submit daily or weekly reports, upload documents, and communicate with supervisors online. Supervisors can log in to monitor student progress, review reports, provide comments, and assess performance remotely. Administrators can also manage records and generate reports easily.
The introduction of such a system will reduce dependence on paper records and improve the speed of information sharing. It will also promote accountability, transparency, and proper documentation. Students will find it easier to keep track of their activities, while supervisors will be able to monitor students more effectively regardless of distance. Institutions will benefit from better data storage and easier access to information.
Therefore, this project focuses on the design and implementation of a Student SIWES Activity Tracking System that will provide an efficient and reliable means of managing SIWES activities in tertiary institutions.
1.2 Statement of Problem
The Students Industrial Work Experience Scheme is an important academic 
program
, but its management in many institutions is still faced with several challenges due to the use of manual processes. These problems affect students, supervisors, and the institution as a whole.
One major problem is the difficulty in monitoring students who are attached to different organizations and locations. Since students may be posted to various states and cities, physical supervision becomes expensive, stressful, and time-consuming. Some students may not be visited regularly during the training period.
Another problem is delay in submission and review of reports. Students often submit their logbooks and reports at the end of the program, making it difficult for supervisors to identify problems early or provide timely corrections.
Poor communication is also a challenge. Students may need guidance during their training, but reaching supervisors may not be easy. This can affect productivity and learning outcomes.
There is also the issue of loss, damage, or falsification of records. Paper logbooks can be misplaced, destroyed, or altered, which reduces the reliability of information presented by students.
In addition, manual record keeping makes it difficult for institutions to maintain accurate databases of students on SIWES, their places of attachment, supervisors, and performance records. Retrieving information for decision-making or future reference may be difficult.
These challenges highlight the need for an automated Student SIWES Activity Tracking System to improve supervision, reporting, communication, and data management.
1.3 Aim and Objectives of the Study
The main aim of this study is to design and implement a Student SIWES Activity Tracking System.
The specific objectives of the study are to:
Design a secure platform for managing and tracking students’ SIWES activities
Develop a secure digital platform for managing and tracking students’ SIWES activities
Provide a module for students to key in daily tasks, upload evidence and submit progress reports
Enable supervisors review, comment on and approve students’ SIWES activities remotely
Automate the generation and storage of SIWES logs and performance reports, in order to reduce the inefficiencies associated with manual SIWES logbook management
1.4 Significance of the Study
This project will be beneficial to students, supervisors, institutions, and researchers.
Students: The system will make it easier for students to record and submit their SIWES activities regularly. It will also help them receive quick feedback from supervisors.
Supervisors: Supervisors will be able to monitor students more effectively without depending entirely on physical visits. They can review reports and assess performance online.
Institutions: Schools will benefit from proper record keeping, easier access to information, and better management of SIWES operations.
Government and Regulatory Bodies: Agencies responsible for educational standards can use accurate SIWES data for planning and policy development.
Researchers: The study will serve as useful reference material for future students and researchers in related areas.
1.5 Scope of the Study
This study covers the design and implementation of a Student SIWES Activity Tracking System for tertiary institutions. The system will focus on the following functions:
Student registration and login
Upload of SIWES details and place of attachment
Submission of daily or weekly activity reports
Supervisor monitoring and feedback
Administrative management of records
Report generation and documentation
The study is limited to the management and tracking of SIWES activities and does not cover payroll systems or detailed company operations where students are attached.

2.2 Theoretical Background
 
This section gives a theoretical background for the design and implementation of the Student SIWES Activity Tracking System. It is divided into two sub-sections. The first part discusses the major concepts 
supporting
 the development of the system, while the second highlights the underlying technologies employed in developing the system. 
2.
2
.1 Concepts
 
Students' Industrial Work Experience Scheme (SIWES)
: SIWES is a skills-training program established in Nigeria by the Industrial Training Fund (ITF) in 1973 to reduce the gap between the knowledge acquired theoretically in schools and the skills needed in industry. SIWES is a tripartite arrangement involving the Student, the Institution and the Industry (Host Employer), where ITF serves as the coordinating and financing agency. 
Logbook/Activity Log
: Traditionally, SIWES involves the use of a logbook which is a physical booklet where the student records the activities he or she undertakes everyday. This includes the tasks undertaken, the skills acquired and the challenges met during the industrial attachment period. The logbook is reviewed and signed off by the supervisor(s) and used to evaluate the performance of the student. For this project, the logbook is replaced with an "activity log" which is an electronic record stored in a database that makes it impossible to enter records retroactively. 
Role-Based Access Control (RBAC)
: RBAC is a method of restricting user access to system resources based on their roles in the organization. In the multi-user system described in this project, RBAC enables students, school-based supervisors, industry supervisors, institution coordinators, and ITF officers to only view the data and execute the functions pertinent to them. 
Web-Based Information System
: Web-based information system is an application accessed via a web browser over the internet where presentation, processing and storage of data are logically separated into three different layers. Through this design, the system can be accessed remotely by various users without the installation of any local software.
2.2.2 Technologies
React.js
: React.js is a JavaScript library for building interactive user interfaces through reusable components. It is used in this project to implement the presentation layer, providing a responsive interface accessible from both desktop and mobile browsers.
Node.js and Express.js: Node.js is a JavaScript runtime environment that allows JavaScript to be executed outside a web browser, commonly used to build the server-side (back-end) logic of web applications. Express.js is a minimal and flexible Node.js framework used to build the RESTful Application Programming Interface (API) that mediates communication between the front-end and the database in this project.
MySQL
: MySQL is an open-source relational database management system (RDBMS) that organizes data into structured tables with defined relationships. It is adopted in this project as the data layer, storing student, supervisor, activity log, placement, and notification records with enforced referential integrity.
JSON Web Tokens (JWT) and Role-Based Authentication
: JWT is a compact, self-contained means of securely transmitting information between parties as a digitally signed JSON object. In this project, JWT is used to authenticate users statelessly and to encode role information used for authorization decisions across the five actor categories.
2.3 Review of Related Literature
This section reviews six closely related studies on the design and implementation of web-based systems for SIWES and internship management. Each study is summarized in terms of its aim, methodology and findings, followed by a statement of its relationship, similarities, and differences with the present study.
Onihunwa et al. (2020) developed the Obong SIWES Online Interactive Platform, a web portal built with HTML, CSS, Bootstrap, SQL, and JavaScript, and designed using a modified Waterfall model. The platform allowed students to fill and submit their logbooks and final reports electronically, while supervisors could access and grade logbook entries at any point during the training period, and the SIWES coordinator could monitor overall progress. The study concluded that the portal successfully digitized the paper-based logbook process for the case institution studied. This work is closely related to the present study in that both digitize the SIWES logbook and provide differentiated access for students and supervisors. However, the Obong SIWES portal was built with a simpler, static-page technology stack (PHP/HTML/Bootstrap) rather than a modern three-tier, API-driven architecture, and it modeled only three broad actor categories (Student, Supervisor/IBS, and SIWES Coordinator/ITF) without granular role separation. It also did not incorporate timestamped submission specifically to prevent backdated entries, automated notifications, or exportable reports, all of which are addressed in the proposed Student SIWES Activity Tracking System.
Adetiba et al. (2012) developed the e-SIWES Portal, a web-based platform intended to enhance the manual processes of SIWES registration, information dissemination, logbook completion, and supervision/assessment by lecturers and industry-based supervisors. The portal digitized the logbook and assessment forms and allowed supervisors to be assigned to students and to monitor them in near real time, in addition to enabling broadcast messaging to all students at once. Like the present study, the e-SIWES Portal is a web-based platform aimed at eliminating the inefficiencies of the manual, paper-based SIWES logbook and assessment process. It differs, however, in that it was developed over a decade ago using an older technology stack and does not incorporate modern security mechanisms such as password hashing or token-based authentication, structured role-based access control across five distinct actor categories, or automated, event-triggered email notifications, all of which form part of the proposed system's design.
Afolayan and Onoriode (2025) designed and implemented a secure, role-based, web-enabled SIWES logbook system at the Federal University of Technology, Akure (FUTA), following the Waterfall Software Development Life Cycle. The system adopted a four-tier architecture comprising a React.js presentation layer, a Node.js application layer, a MySQL data layer, and a dedicated security layer employing Bcrypt password hashing, JSON Web Tokens (JWT) for stateless authentication, and Role-Based Access Control (RBAC). A usability and security evaluation involving 40 participants found that a majority of users were satisfied with the system, rated it easy to navigate, considered it secure, and preferred it over the paper-based logbook. 
This study is the most technologically similar to the present work, sharing the same core stack
; 
React.js, Node.js, and MySQL — as well as JWT-based authentication and role-based access control. The key differences are that the FUTA system was deployed only in a temporary test environment for a single institution and was not integrated with the Industrial Training Fund (ITF) for national-level verification or stipend eligibility, whereas the proposed Student SIWES Activity Tracking System explicitly incorporates an ITF Officer role with cross-institutional, read-only access for verification and reporting purposes, along with exportable PDF reports and automated email notifications tied to workflow events.
Jaafar et al. (2017), in a study carried out at Universiti Teknologi MARA (UiTM), Malaysia, developed a web-based system for monitoring and supervising student internships. The system computerized internship eligibility checking, registration, industry visit scheduling, and monitoring of the internship 
program
, making these processes accessible online rather than requiring physical, in-person coordination. This study is related to the present work in its shared objective of replacing manual, visit-dependent supervision with an online monitoring system. It differs substantially in scope and context: it was developed for the Malaysian industrial training system rather than the Nigerian SIWES/ITF framework, it emphasizes eligibility checking and visit scheduling rather than daily, timestamped activity logging, and it does not model the five distinct actor roles (Student, School-based Supervisor, Industry Supervisor, Institution Coordinator, and ITF Officer) that structure the proposed system's role-based dashboards.
Nurfaizi and Hindarto (2023) developed a Web-Based Student Internship Attendance Application System, designed using the Waterfall method, to streamline the recording of student internship 
attendance. The system allowed students to record daily attendance through mobile devices or computers and to submit reports on their daily assignments and experiences, while giving 
program
 organizers instantaneous access to attendance data for management and evaluation purposes. This work shares with the present study the goal of replacing manual, paper-based recording with a web-based, timestamped system that supports daily reporting by students. However, its scope is narrower, concentrating specifically on attendance monitoring rather than a full activity-logging and multi-stage supervisor review-and-approval workflow. It also does not model a multi-actor, role-based structure comparable to the five actor categories in the proposed system, nor does it address stipend or funding-body verification, which is central to the Nigerian SIWES context addressed by this project.
Ele et al. (2017) examined the semi-automated and largely manual nature of SIWES registration and stipend payment processes in Nigeria, under which students queue physically for registration and again for the disbursement of ITF stipends. In response, the authors designed and implemented the SIWES Registration and Payment System (SIWES-RPS), a web-based platform intended for use by students, institutional supervisors, and SIWES-based supervisors to computerize registration and payment documentation and reduce the associated delays. This study is closely related to the present work in its identification of manual registration and stipend-payment inefficiencies within the Nigerian SIWES/ITF system, which the proposed Student SIWES Activity Tracking System also addresses through its ITF Officer role for stipend eligibility validation. The SIWES-RPS, however, focuses specifically on registration and payment processing rather than daily activity logging and supervisor review, and it does not provide the real-time, timestamped logbook functionality, role-based dashboards for five distinct actor categories, or automated notification system that form the core of the proposed system.
2.4 Research/Literature/Knowledge Gap
The literature reviewed shows that a substantial body of work exists on the digitization of SIWES and internship management processes in Nigeria and beyond, ranging from early web-based portals (Onihunwa et al., 2020; Adetiba et al., 2012) and registration/payment-focused systems (Sylvester et al., 2017), to narrower attendance-tracking systems (Nurfaizi & Hindarto, 2023) and 
internship-supervision systems developed outside the Nigerian SIWES/ITF framework (Jaafar et al., 2017). More recent work has begun to incorporate modern security practices such as password hashing and token-based, role-based authentication (Afolayan & Onoriode, 2025), using a technology stack closely comparable to that adopted in this project.
Nevertheless, none of the reviewed studies combines, in a single system, all of the following: (i) a modern, secure, three-tier React.js/Node.js/MySQL architecture with JWT-based, role-based access control; (ii) timestamped, tamper-resistant daily activity logging designed specifically to prevent backdated or fraudulent entries; (iii) differentiated, role-based dashboards for five distinct actor categories ; Student, School-based Supervisor, Industry Supervisor, Institution Coordinator, and ITF Officer, reflecting the full tripartite structure of the Nigerian SIWES scheme; (iv) an ITF Officer role with cross-institutional, read-only access for logbook verification and stipend eligibility validation; and (v) automated, event-triggered email notifications together with exportable PDF reporting for institutional and ITF submission.
This gap constitutes the specific contribution of the present study. The Student SIWES Activity Tracking System is designed to integrate secure, modern authentication and role-based access control with comprehensive, multi-actor activity tracking and ITF-level verification within a single, unified, web-based platform, thereby addressing the combined weaknesses of fraudulent logbook entries, inadequate supervision, loss of records, delayed stipend payments, and poor cross-stakeholder communication identified in the existing SIWES literature.

 Methodology
Several software development methodologies exist for the analysis and design of information systems. 
The most common of them are SSADM (Structured Systems Analysis and Design Method), OOADM (Object Oriented Analysis and Design Methodology), Agile/Scrum approach, Waterfall model, and Prototype model. Each of them has their own features, benefits and limitations that need to be considered regarding the needs of the project.
The SSADM is a structured approach to modeling systems that uses DFD (Data Flow Diagram) and ERD (Entity Relationship Diagram) models of the systems. It is best used when working on data oriented, batch processing systems where process flows are well defined and linear. Similar to it, the Waterfall model is inflexible and cannot handle iteration after certain stage completion. The Agile/Scrum approach, although highly flexible and user-centered, is better used for rapidly changing requirements environment.
The Object Oriented Analysis and Design Methodology (OOADM), based on the Unified Modeling Language (UML), has been chosen to develop this project. The reason for choosing this methodology is that this system is role-based and actor-oriented, there are many actors involved in the processes in the system – Students, School-based Supervisors, Industry Supervisors, Institution Coordinators and ITF Officers, each one has his/her own role and interaction. OOADM models the system as set of interacting objects that makes this methodology highly effective for modeling the multi actor system.
Using OOADM approach in this project gives us an opportunity to use UML diagrams – Use Case Diagram, Activity Diagram, Class Diagram, Sequence Diagram which provide us with complete graphical description of the system structure and behavior. This methodology will be implemented in the following stages – requirements analysis (Use Case and Activity Diagrams), design (Class and Sequence Diagrams and database structure) and implementation.

 Systems Analysis
Systems analysis is the process of studying an existing system to identify its strengths and weaknesses, with the goal of designing a superior replacement. This section presents a description of the existing SIWES management system and an analysis of the proposed system.
3.
3
.1 Description of the Existing System
The management of the Students' Industrial Work Experience Scheme (SIWES) currently in Nigerian higher education institutions, including University of Nigeria, Nsukka, is highly manual and based on paper. Students are provided with paper log books right from the start of their industrial training. The log books are supposed to be written daily in terms of what activities were carried out by the student, what he/she learned and what problems he/she encountered in his/her place of attachment.
Periodically during the duration of training, a school-based supervisor is supposed to go physically to the place of attachment of the student to check whether the log book entries are true, meet the industry supervisor and evaluate the student. After the completion of training, the student presents his/her log book to the departmental SIWES coordinator, who then submits it to ITF for validation and stipend payment.
Institutional coordinators maintain manual records of student placements, and all communication between the student, supervisors, institution, and the ITF is conducted via physical documentation, verbal interactions, or informal digital channels such as email and phone calls. The existing system provides no structured digital platform for real-time activity tracking, role-based access to records, or automated reporting.

3.
3
.1.1 Weaknesses of the Existing System
A thorough study of the existing SIWES management system reveals the following critical weaknesses:
Fraudulent Logbook Entries: Since students fill paper logbooks manually and supervisors can only visit once or twice during the training period, it is practically impossible to verify whether entries are made daily or filled retroactively at the end of the training period. This compromises the integrity of the scheme.
Inadequate Supervision: School-based supervisors face logistical and financial constraints in physically visiting students at remote or distant attachment locations. This results in most students receiving little to no in-person supervision throughout the training.
Loss and Damage of Records: Physical logbooks are susceptible to loss, damage, or deterioration, leading to permanent loss of student records that are required for ITF verification and stipend payment.
Delayed and Failed Stipend Payments: Non-verification of logbooks, late submission of placement lists, and missing bank details — all consequences of the manual system — result in delayed or failed ITF allowance payments to students.
Lack of Real-Time Monitoring: Neither the institution, the school-based supervisor, nor the ITF has any mechanism for monitoring a student's daily activities in real time during the training period.
Inefficient Communication: Information dissemination among students, supervisors, institution coordinators, and the ITF relies on informal channels, causing delays and miscommunication.
Poor Data Management: The manual collation of placement lists, logbooks, bank details, and assessment forms by SIWES coordinators is error-prone and time-consuming, particularly given the large number of students involved each academic year.
3.
3
.2 Analysis of the Proposed System
The proposed Student SIWES Activity Tracking System is a web-based platform designed to digitize, streamline, and enhance the management of SIWES activities across all stakeholders. The system introduces a role-based architecture that grants differentiated access and functionality to each category of user — Students, School-based Supervisors, Industry Supervisors, Institution Coordinators, and ITF Officers — based on their defined responsibilities within the scheme.
The system will operate on a three-tier web architecture comprising a presentation layer (React.js front-end), an application logic layer (Node.js/Express.js back-end REST API), and a data layer (MySQL relational database). The platform will be accessible via standard web browsers and is designed to be responsive for use on both desktop and mobile devices.
Key functionalities of the proposed system include:
Student daily activity logging with timestamped entries, eliminating the possibility of backdated or fraudulent submissions.
Role-based dashboards for all five actor categories, each displaying relevant data, actions, and notifications.
Real-time supervisor access to student logbook entries, enabling prompt review, comments, and approval or rejection of daily logs.
Digital supervisor visit scheduling and confirmation, replacing the uncoordinated physical visit process.
Institutional coordinator oversight of all students registered under their institution, with placement management and report generation capabilities.
ITF Officer access to cross-institutional student data for logbook verification and stipend eligibility validation.
Automated email notifications to relevant parties upon key events such as log submission, supervisor comment, and approval status updates.
Exportable reports in PDF format for institutional records and ITF submission.

.3 Instantiation of the Model
The proposed system adopts a layered web application architecture model comprising three principal tiers: the Presentation Layer, the Business Logic Layer, and the Data Layer. This three-tier model is a well-established architectural pattern for enterprise web systems, offering clear separation of concerns, improved maintainability, and scalability.
Presentation Layer: Built with React.js, this layer handles all user interface components and user interactions. It communicates with the backend exclusively through RESTful API calls.
Business Logic Layer: Implemented with Node.js and the Express.js framework, this layer processes all application logic, enforces role-based access control (RBAC), handles authentication via JSON Web Tokens (JWT), and manages communication between the front-end and the database.
Data Layer: MySQL serves as the relational database management system. All entities, relationships, and constraints are formally defined in the schema presented in Section 3.3.3.

Foreign key relationships enforce referential integrity across all tables, ensuring that activity logs are always linked to valid students and that supervisor assignments reference existing supervisor records.
3.
4.4
 
Input Design
The input design of the SIWES Activity Tracking System emphasizes accuracy and timely data collection through carefully designed web-based forms tailored for each specific user role. The main source of data will be the activity log provided by students every day. Here, the student must fill in the date, describe his or her activities and note the skills gained. Importantly, the submission of this log will be automatically timestamped, thus precluding any fraudulent data submissions. 
Other inputs to the system will be the registration data and the assignment of a supervisor provided by the Institution Coordinator, along with the decision to approve activities, feedback comments and scheduling data of visits provided by school- and industry-based supervisors. 
3.
4
.5 
Output Design
The output design allows for easy information presentation, timely communication and formal documentation. The main outputs in terms of information presentation will be the role-based dashboards that will show data that is relevant for each user based on his or her access level. For improved communication, automated email notifications will be generated for certain events like submitting logs and results of supervisor evaluation. The system will also generate PDF documents that can be exported and used as the required output document for record-keeping and verification of ITF logbook and stipends.

About OOADM
Object-Oriented Analysis and Design Methodology (OOADM) is an approach to software engineering that models a system as a collection of interacting objects. Each object is an instance of a class, encapsulating both data (attributes) and behaviour (methods). OOADM provides a natural mapping between real-world entities and software constructs, making it highly suitable for systems with complex actor interactions such as the SIWES Activity Tracking System.
